Scorecard

Every number here is graded against a simulated distribution network whose true connectivity, degradation and faults are known exactly. No model reads that truth, it exists only to mark the answers. The inputs are what an Indian DISCOM already owns today: smart-meter voltage, transformer telemetry, last-gasp messages, and a connectivity ledger that is wrong about a third of the time.

Fault Location

A last-gasp burst says something broke. This says where: the fault sits below every dark meter and above every lit one, which brackets it to a span of line measured in ohms from the transformer. That bracket is what a crew walks instead of the whole feeder.

07

Neighbourhood Flexibility

The challenge asks for clean power made dependable street by street, using forecasting, demand response and shared storage. None of those can be aimed at a network nobody has mapped. Forecasts run per phase, because a feeder is almost never in trouble as a whole, one phase is overloaded while another sits idle. Every demand response call list is filtered to the consumers actually on the transformer and phase in trouble.

07

Solar & Voltage

Rooftop PV reverses power flow at midday on feeders built to move it one way. The rise trips inverters and ages transformers. Excursions coincident with export are separated from ordinary load-driven sag, and reactive setpoints go to the inverters deepest on the affected phase, where each kvar buys the most volts. Curtailment is a last resort. It costs the customer generation.
